# Code review — 2026-10-26

Scope: the whole repository at `e6ffc6eed` (main). That covers:
- every tracked source file and the tests;
- the stdlib's Graphix sources, the examples and the book;
- the IDE tooling and the `graphix-lang` skill.

The review also re-ran the 457 repros that the 2026-10-19 review saved. It
paid special attention to the work landed since then that has not soaked yet:
- seq steps reading the world as it stood at their entry, and `live(e)`;
- banked sampling for `~`;
- reference lifetimes (`reference::Lives`) and writes through bottom addresses;
- wake catch-up by write stamp, and guards that sleep when the chain does not
  reach them;
- redelivery of replies an interrupted cycle kept from every reader;
- the rank-2 quantifier rules;
- cancel-safe async evals, queuefn credits, and the io stream types split per
  direction;
- `str::parse` and `json::read` keeping exact numbers, and `filter_map`'s `` `Keep ``;
- end-of-line and below-the-list comments.

Walk pruning and kind markers, built 10-18, are still unsoaked and got their
own reviewers again. `../netidx` was out of scope except where Graphix reaches
it.

## How to read this

- Every finding has an id, such as `c-pattern-01` or `x-seq-sampling-03`. Its
  CR in the code ends with that id:
  `// CR claude for <eric|claude>: [tag/severity] text (id)`.
  - Eric's queue: `cr list --for eric`.
  - One severity: `cr list --tag bug --min-sev high`.
  - One finding: `cr show c-pattern-01`.
- Tags:
  - `bug`: wrong behaviour reachable today.
  - `risk`: a hazard not yet shown to be reachable.
  - `test-gap`, `doc-drift`, `structure`, `perf`, `readability`, `style`,
    `dead`.

  The severity is the reviewer's.
- **No finding was independently verified.** As in the 10-19 round, there was
  no verifier phase, by Eric's decision. Each reviewer was told to confirm by
  running wherever a program could reach the code, and to drop what it could
  not substantiate. Each CR says how its finding was established:
  - `probe: design/review-2026-10-26/repro/<file>` means the reviewer ran it
    and saved the repro.
  - "Found by reading; not run." means it came from the code alone.

  477 of the 701 findings were shown by running a program. All 62 critical
  and high findings were. I re-ran two of the critical repros myself
  (f-mod-lowering-01 and c-pattern-01), and both reproduce.
- Repros are in `design/review-2026-10-26/repro/`. Each file's header gives:
  - the command;
  - the expected output;
  - what HEAD prints.

  Most run as `graphix --no-cache <file>` or `graphix-fuzz check <file>`. A
  `.rs` repro is an integration test: copy it into the named crate's `tests/`
  to run it. The directory also holds probes that no CR cites.
- Duplicates were merged mechanically when they had the same file, a nearby
  line and a similar title. The kept CR says "Also reported as (…)". One root
  cause reported at different sites keeps a CR at each site; the themes below
  name the clusters.
- Drift in `CLAUDE.md` is not written as CRs. It is listed near the end of
  this document.

## How the review was done

The kit is the 10-19 round's, adapted to today's tree. The tree was cut into
91 units of about 2–4k lines each, and one reviewer read each unit line by
line. 31 more reviewers each took one concern across the whole repository:
- **Adversarial semantic probes:**
  - firing and wake;
  - collections and kernels;
  - seq, errors and references;
  - generics;
  - patterns;
  - walk pruning;
  - kind markers;
  - three new this round: seq snapshots and banked sampling; references,
    write-stamp catch-up and redelivery; async cancel safety, queuefn and the
    stream types.
- **Robustness and contracts:**
  - stack depth;
  - images;
  - unsafe code;
  - panics;
  - security;
  - concurrency;
  - the node trait contract;
  - builtin effect classes;
  - parallel evaluation.
- **Performance, measured:**
  - compile-time scaling;
  - run-time scaling.
- **Tools and hygiene:**
  - the formatter;
  - the language server, driven over stdio;
  - duplication;
  - doc drift.
- **Re-runs:** six reviewers re-ran the 457 repros of the 10-19 review. Each
  classified its repros as fixed, still reproducing, regressed or obsolete,
  and tried a variant of each narrow fix.

There were 122 agents and about 40M subagent tokens. Two incidents affected
the run:
- **The probe wrapper leaked processes.** Early on, `timeout` killed the probe
  wrapper but not the binary under it. Leaked processes filled the probe
  slice's task limit, so new probes failed to spawn threads (EAGAIN). The
  wrapper was fixed (`setpriv --pdeathsig KILL`) and the leaked processes were
  killed. The x-stack reviewer re-ran its affected probes. No finding rests on
  an EAGAIN panic. Two findings came out of the incident itself:
  - x-perf-runtime-04 and x-perf-compile-06: the evaluation pool's promised
    fallback to fewer workers panics instead. That fallback is real code.
- **The spend limit.** 26 reviewers hit the monthly spend limit. They were
  re-run to completion once it was raised.

## Overall

The engines are in good health. The cross-cutting reviewers said so in nearly
every area:
- **Engine agreement:** about 300 adversarial semantic probes covered firing,
  collections, seq, references, pruning, kind markers, sampling and catch-up.
  Every one agreed across:
  - node-walk and JIT;
  - cold and warm images;
  - forked and serial;
  - pruned and unpruned.

  The defects these reviewers found are in semantics both engines share, or in
  the type system.
- **Walk pruning:** marking is thorough. Every this-cycle write that was
  traced reaches `mark_readers`. The pruning bugs come from state that records
  *whether a walk happened* (see theme 4).
- **Unsafe code:** every safety contract traced is upheld, including the new
  arena, relocation and `ParLoan`.
- **Images:** cold and warm runs agree, and staleness holds in every case
  tried. `--warm` passes over all 129 book examples.
- **Formatter:** idempotent over all 2,482 `.gx`/`.gxi` files in both repos,
  at many widths and indents and with CRLF. Every refusal is a parse error.
  Most of the formatter findings are comment placements the new grammar
  accepts in one place and refuses in another (theme 8).
- **Typechecker speed:** linear and fast on real code. The largest examples
  check in 35–85 ms, and netidx-admin re-checks in 0.14 s per edit in the LSP.
- **The 10-19 fixes:** of the 457 repros, 424 are fixed and 16 are held by
  open CRs. The rest are helper scripts or obsolete, except one generator
  template that regressed in a new way. 37 findings come from these re-runs,
  almost all of them variants of narrow fixes that fail at another site of the
  same rule.

The defects cluster in four places:
- the type checker's handling of unions under constructors, hidden labeled
  defaults and rank-2 types;
- async calls meeting a sleep or a seq abort;
- the data boundary: map keys, union reads, `str::parse`;
- untrusted-input hardening in the small format packages.

## Themes

1. **Unsound typing that reaches the JIT (critical).** Six critical findings,
   each checked by a run:
   - **Unions nested under a constructor narrow to their first member:**
     c-pattern-01. This gives a wrong arm, false refusals and a JIT panic.
     Neighbours are c-pattern-02/03/04/05/06, x-rerun-2-01 and x-rerun-3-05.
   - **Slice patterns type each element apart but test only the element
     union at run time:** x-typecheck-patterns-01, which is UB in the JIT.
     Nearby are x-typecheck-patterns-02/03/06 and t-parser-b-01.
   - **A hidden labeled default is judged without the return type:**
     x-typecheck-generics-01 and tests-functions-01. An `i64` is typed as a
     string and the JIT aborts. c-callsite-02 is the same rule inside a union
     member.
   - **A forward type name in a `let` binds the outer typedef but is checked
     against the block's:** t-env-01, which is JIT type confusion.
   - **The kernel cache key ignores callees' `?` handlers:**
     f-mod-lowering-01. A raise reaches another call's catch.
2. **The check and the build disagree.** Each of these is accepted by
   `--check` and refused by elaboration, or the reverse:
   - x-typecheck-generics-03/04/05;
   - c-callsite-02, c-error-op-01, t-contains-01;
   - c-module-traits-02, t-env-02;
   - x-refs-catchup-01 and c-bind-01: `&mut` of an inferred or
     operator-initialized local is refused at every run-time bind.

   The rank-2 rules are new and have gaps:
   - t-fntyp-01..04;
   - x-rerun-3-01: a rank-2 type nested in a struct, array or union formal is
     unchecked;
   - t-tvar-01: generic scratch cells become live types.
3. **Async calls meeting a sleep or a seq abort.** `CANCEL_SAFE` is declared
   for evals that run to completion anyway, so a sleep drops a reply whose
   effect already happened:
   - a popped db entry or a commit's outcome: db2-01, x-builtin-effects-01,
     http-sqlite-db1-01;
   - a spawned child that is orphaned: sys-io-01, x-concurrency-02.

   `CachedArgsAsync` has no `reborn`, so a reply kept from an aborted seq run
   answers the next run's step: core-lib-01, x-async-io-02 and
   x-seq-sampling-04. Also in this theme:
   - x-async-io-01/03/04/05, the read, close and queuefn edges;
   - core-aux-01/02;
   - x-concurrency-01: `sys::exit` after a db insert loses the insert.
4. **State that records whether a walk happened.** The engines agree, but
   pruned and unpruned runs do not:
   - x-prune-01 and c-prune-wake-01/02: `TrackedFires::last_run` and the
     write-stamp `owed` measure against the last walk, not the last cycle the
     arm was awake. Pruning then re-raises phantom fires.
   - Catch-up gaps: tests-select-01/03/04, x-engine-firing-03 and
     x-refs-catchup-05.
   - x-refs-catchup-07: interrupt redelivery treats "visited boundary" as
     "seen", so it misses builtin slot callees.
   - x-prune-02: a dynamic module inside a `let` is forked beside the readers
     of its exports.
   - c-analysis-branch-01/02: a forked `*p <- v` or `==` over references reads
     a stale place.
5. **Seq snapshots and banked sampling (new work).** The entry-snapshot rule
   mostly holds, but it breaks in these places:
   - **Captures go live:** a seqq step that calls a builtin taking a callback,
     or that holds a nested seq, makes every capture live. See
     x-seq-sampling-01, x-dup-01 and t-seq-01.
   - **Reads at the wrong time:**
     - x-seq-sampling-03: `*r <- v` reads `r` when `v` fires.
     - tests-lang-rest-b-04: the snapshot is joint, not per variable, which
       CLAUDE.md does not say.
     - t-seq-03/05: a nullary call is a level read, and a step waits for every
       variable its text reads.
   - **Requests run with the wrong value:** requests banked while a capture is
     absent all run with the last request's value (x-seq-sampling-02,
     tests-seq-01).
   - **Lost triggers:** banked `~` drops a trigger that finds its argument
     bottom after a value (x-engine-firing-01, x-seq-sampling-05,
     c-node-mod-01).
   - **Flush:** seqq `flush` is dropped between queued runs
     (x-engine-seq-errors-01).
   - **Generator:** fuzz-gen-b-01: the generator's `until` waits now read the
     entry snapshot, so most generated seq runs stall. The fuzzer is
     exercising much less seq than it appears to.
6. **`filter_map`'s `` `Keep ``.** These findings need Eric's ruling:
   - x-engine-collections-01/02, c-collection-01 and gx-stdlib-01:
     `filter_map` unwraps any `` `Keep(v) ``. So a `'b` that itself holds
     `` `Keep `` is not type-safe, and `told()` waives the runtime-form check
     that would refuse it.
7. **The data boundary.** The in-memory forms are sound. These findings are
   where data leaves or enters:
   - **Map keys:** json, toml and hbs write composite, variant, null and bytes
     map keys as quoted text that no reader takes back. This is a regression
     for enum-keyed maps. See x-kind-markers-01, t-rep-02, core-lib-02,
     x-rerun-4-03 and x-rerun-5-04.
   - **Union reads pick the wrong member:** x-kind-markers-02/03/04,
     t-cast-setops-06, tests-types-01 and x-rerun-5-03.
   - **Exponential backtracking:** t-cast-setops-02: a 41-byte JSON stalls
     `json::read` for 6.6 s.
   - **`str::parse` cannot read what Graphix prints** for durations,
     datetimes, bytes, NaN and inf: collections-str-02, t-expr-core-01 and
     x-rerun-2-02.
   - **Exact numbers:** x-kind-markers-05/06 and small-pkgs-06.
   - **Untyped foreign data:** sys-net-01: rpc and write refusals carry marked
     variants, so netidx drops the caller's whole connection. x-security-01: a
     netidx write of an error value reaches a typed `#on_write` handler.
8. **Comment placement (new grammar).** End-of-line and below-the-list comments
   are accepted in blocks but refused in:
   - interfaces, trait and impl bodies, and map entries;
   - after a trailing `;`;
   - comment-only files.

   They are also accepted in string splices, where the printer cannot keep
   them. One case loses a comment silently: tests-expr-03, a comment inside an
   attribute argument, which the reparse guard does not see. See
   t-parser-a-01..04, t-parser-b-02/03, x-formatter-01..03, tests-parser-01,
   tests-expr-02/03 and t-format-resolver-01.
9. **Untrusted input in the format packages.** Several small files take the
   process down:
   - a 1 KB xlsx and a 5.6 KB xls: small-pkgs-02/03/04;
   - a crafted hbs template overflows the stack: small-pkgs-01, x-rerun-5-05;
   - 1.2 MB of crafted `pack::read` bytes: x-security-03.

   Separately, x-security-05: the image cache runs an entry that anyone with
   write access to the cache could forge. There is no owner or mode check,
   and the CRC32 is forgeable. That needs a policy from Eric.
10. **Stack depth.**
    - x-stack-01 and t-rep-01: a `db::insert` of a 300-element `List` aborts
      the process, because `to_marked_data` recurses per list cell without a
      guard.
    - x-stack-02: the GUI widget compiler lacks the guard the TUI got.
    - x-stack-03: place paths.
    - x-perf-compile-01/07: an owned-array fold chain grows the kernel frame
      quadratically and aborts at about 700 lets, and no guard exists for a
      kernel frame larger than the red zone.
    - tests-shell-compiler-01: a warm start of nested lambda literals
      overflows a 512K stack.
11. **The fuzzer's own accuracy.** Some of these hide divergences, and some
    report false ones:
    - fuzz-gen-b-01: the seq stall above;
    - fuzz-lib-a-01..04: FinalValues drops later prints, a construction
      timeout agrees with any containment, and a failed image write is hidden;
    - fuzz-lib-b-01/02;
    - fuzz-gen-a-01/02;
    - fuzz-mutate-01/02.
12. **Performance.**
    - x-builtin-effects-02: fused kernels rerun a fast call whose arguments
      did not fire, so heavy fast functions run 23x slower fused.
    - x-perf-compile: quadratic SSA aliases from scope-drop `use_var`, and
      O(N²) union normalization.
    - x-stack-04: a recursion with a catch per activation is quadratic.
    - The long-seq O(S²) and the per-input cost of idle selects still
      reproduce (x-perf-runtime).
13. **The GUI and TUI** have edge bugs:
    - a plotters panic at chrono's end (gui-chart-01);
    - canvas tessellation that is unclipped (gui-widgets-a-01);
    - stdout and stderr drawn over the TUI frame (tui-core-01/02);
    - u16 clamps in paragraph scroll and the calendar (tui-widgets-01/04);
    - window refires that undo a user resize (gui-core-01).
